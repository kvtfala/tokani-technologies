The Tokani approach

Understand the problem. Keep the solution practical.

Every project starts with the business, not a product catalogue. We learn how things work today before deciding what should change.

Understand the business

We learn how customers find you, how enquiries are handled, what information matters, where time is lost and what outcome you want.

Identify the right stage

We decide whether you need a foundation, digitisation for growth, or a custom solution — and we avoid adding systems you are not ready to use.

Agree the scope

We define what is included, the pages or processes involved, the services being configured, and what sits outside the agreed package.

Build & configure

Tokani builds the website or system, connects the agreed tools and configures the workflows required for the approved implementation.

Launch & hand over

We test, deploy and provide initial handover or training so the business can confidently use the solution in normal operations.

Grow when it makes sense

As your business changes, we can assess new workflows, users, integrations or custom requirements and scope the next sensible step.

A practical principle

Tokani builds and configures the system. The business operates through it.

For Tubu, the goal is to give an SME a working customer-management system — not turn Tokani into an outsourced day-to-day CRM administrator.

What we look at during onboarding

How enquiries enter the business.

What information needs to be captured.

How enquiries are qualified and followed up.

How quotations, bookings or sales are confirmed.

What simple automation would remove repetitive work.

What “practical” means
01

Business outcome first

Technology is explained only to the level needed to make a confident decision.

02

Clear boundaries

Up to, standard, basic, agreed, and configured during implementation — scope stays understandable.

03

Honest recommendations

If a tool, feature or investment is unnecessary or premature, we should say so.

04

Room to grow

New workflows or complexity can be assessed later instead of being forced into the first project.
